## Q001
**Status:** active
**Type:** true-false
**Difficulty:** introductory
**Topic:** propositions-and-truth-values
**Focus Area:** identifying non-propositions
**Question:** Is "Close the door." a proposition?
**Answer:** No. It is a command, not a proposition. Propositions must be statements that are either true or false; commands do not have a truth value. (See propositions-and-truth-values.md)

## Q016
**Status:** active
**Type:** mcq
**Difficulty:** intermediate
**Topic:** tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence
**Focus Area:** identifying tautologies
**Question:** Which of the following is a tautology?
A) p ∧ ¬p
B) p ∨ ¬p
C) ¬p ∨ ¬q
D) p ∧ q
**Answer:** B) p ∨ ¬p. This is the Law of the Excluded Middle — for any truth value of p, either p is true or ¬p is true, so the statement is always true. A is a contradiction; C and D are only sometimes true (satisfiable). (See tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence.md)

A) p ∨ ¬p
B) p ∧ q
C) p ∧ ¬p
D) p ⟹ q
**Answer:** C) p ∧ ¬p. There is no truth assignment where p and ¬p can both be true, so the statement is false for every row in the truth table. A is a tautology; B and D are satisfiable. (See tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence.md)

## Q018
**Status:** active

**Topic:** tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence
**Focus Area:** logical equivalence definition
**Question:** What does it mean for two propositions to be logically equivalent?
**Answer:** Two propositions p and q are logically equivalent if they have identical truth values in every possible case. Written as p ≡ q. Equivalently, p and q are logically equivalent if the biconditional p ⟺ q is a tautology. (See tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence.md)

## Q020
**Status:** active
**Type:** short-answer
**Difficulty:** advanced
**Topic:** tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence
**Focus Area:** De Morgan's Laws
**Question:** State both of De Morgan's Laws.
**Answer:** ¬(p ∧ q) ≡ ¬p ∨ ¬q, and ¬(p ∨ q) ≡ ¬p ∧ ¬q. Negation distributes through ∧ and ∨ and flips the operator. (See tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence.md)

## Q022
**Status:** active
**Type:** conceptual
**Difficulty:** advanced
**Topic:** tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence
**Focus Area:** Modus Ponens
**Question:** What does Modus Ponens state, and why is it a tautology?
**Answer:** Modus Ponens states: if p is true, and p implies q, then q must also be true. Formally: (p ∧ (p ⟹ q)) ⟹ q. It is a tautology because every row in its truth table evaluates to True — no assignment of truth values makes it false. (See tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence.md)

**Topic:** tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence
**Focus Area:** Law of Excluded Middle
**Question:** Explain why p ∨ ¬p is a tautology and what principle it represents.
**Answer:** p ∨ ¬p is a tautology because regardless of what truth value p has, either p is true or ¬p is true — there is no case where both are false simultaneously. This is the Law of the Excluded Middle: every proposition is either true or false with no "in-between." (See tautologies-contradictions-and-equivalence.md)
