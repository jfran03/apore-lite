# Proof by Contradiction

## Definition
To prove a statement p, assume temporarily that ¬p (the opposite of the statement) is true. Through logical reasoning or algebraic manipulation, show that this assumption leads to a contradiction: a statement that cannot possibly be true (such as 1=0 or a number being both even and odd). Once a contradiction is reached, we conclude that the assumption ¬p must be false, and therefore p must be true.
> Source: Proof by Contradiction.html

## Key Concepts

- **Why It Works:** Every statement is either true or false: there's no middle ground. So, if assuming the opposite of a statement leads to an impossibility, then the only remaining option is that the statement itself must be true.
  > Source: Proof by Contradiction.html

- **When Proof by Contradiction Is Particularly Effective:**
  - A direct proof seems complex or circular.
  - The statement involves words like "impossible," "no," "cannot," or "not."
  - The structure involves negations or inequalities.
  > Source: Proof by Contradiction.html

- **Recognizing When to Use It:** When a problem feels like a trap, where every path from the assumption leads to nonsense, that's often the signal that contradiction is the right tool to use.
  > Source: Proof by Contradiction.html

## Examples

- **√2 is irrational:** The classic example of proof by contradiction is proving that √2 is irrational by assuming it is rational and showing this assumption leads to an impossibility.
  > Source: Proof by Contradiction.html

## Practice Problems

**Prove by contradiction:**
1. There is no largest integer.
2. Let q be a non-zero rational number and r be an irrational number, then qr is irrational.
3. There are no integers n and m such that n² = 4m + 2.
