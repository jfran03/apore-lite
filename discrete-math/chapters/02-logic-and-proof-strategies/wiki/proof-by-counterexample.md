# Proof by Counterexample

## Definition
A proof by counterexample disproves a universal statement (a statement that claims "for all..."). To disprove it, you only need to find one specific example where the statement does not hold true. A universal statement like "For all elements x in a set, property P(x) is true" is only true if every case satisfies the condition. If you can find even one case that violates P(x), the statement fails completely; no further proof is needed.

## Key Concepts

- **When to Use:** This is a simple but powerful tool for disproving incorrect generalizations, especially when:
  - The claim uses words like "all," "every" or "for any."
  - You suspect the claim sounds too broad to be true.
  - It's easy to test specific examples.

## Examples

- **All even numbers are divisible by 4 (false):** Claim: All even numbers are divisible by 4. Note: A number a is divisible by another number b if dividing a by b results in an integer (a whole number with no remainder). Counterexample: Consider 6. It's even because 6 = 2 × 3, but when you divide by 4, 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5, which is not an integer. Because 6 is even but not divisible by 4, the claim fails. Conclusion: The statement "All even numbers are divisible by 4" is false. ∎
  > Source: Proof by Counterexample.html

- **The sum of two irrational numbers is always irrational (false):** Claim: The sum of two irrational numbers is always irrational. Note: A number is irrational if it cannot be written as a fraction a/b where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0. Counterexample: Let x = π and y = −π. Both x and y are irrational, but their sum is x + y = π + (−π) = 0. The result, 0, is rational, because it can be written as 0/1. This single example shows that the claim fails. Conclusion: The sum of two irrational numbers is not always irrational. ∎
  > Source: Proof by Counterexample.html

## Common Misconceptions

- Proof by counterexample reminds us that in logic, universality is fragile: a single exception proves that "always" is too strong, and it only takes one clear example to bring down an entire claim.
