# The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

## Definition

**Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (FTA).** Every integer greater than 1 can be written uniquely as a prime or as a product of prime numbers, where the prime factors are written in non-decreasing order.
> Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html

## Key Concepts

- **Existence:** Every integer greater than 1 can be broken down into primes.
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html
- **Uniqueness:** No matter how you factor it, the prime building blocks are always the same, except for their order. This is a number's unique "DNA."
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html
- **Order does not matter:** Rearranging the order of the prime factors (e.g., 3 × 2 × 7 × 5) does not change the factorization — it is still the same set of primes.
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html

## Finding the Prime Factorization

Process: (1) Start with the smallest prime, 2, and divide the number by 2 as many times as possible. (2) Move to the next prime (3, 5, 7, …) and continue dividing until the quotient becomes 1. (3) Write the result as a product of primes, combining repeated primes using exponents.
> Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html

## Examples

- **24** = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 = 2³ × 3
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html
- **60** = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 2² × 3 × 5
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html
- **91** = 7 × 13; **97** is prime (cannot be factored further).
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html
- **210 — uniqueness demonstrated:** 210 can start as 21 × 10, 15 × 14, or 7 × 30, but every path reduces to the same primes: 210 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 7. Only the fully-prime form meets the conditions of the FTA.
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html
- **7007:** 7007 ÷ 7 = 1001; 1001 ÷ 7 = 143; 143 ÷ 11 = 13; 13 is prime. Final: 7007 = 7 × 7 × 11 × 13 = 7² × 11 × 13.
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html
- **18** = 2 × 3 × 3 is the correct prime factorization (whereas 3 × 6, 2 × 9, or 9 × 2 × 1 are not fully prime).
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Larger numbers have multiple factorizations."** A number can be *started* many ways (e.g., 210 = 21 × 10 = 15 × 14 = 7 × 30), but numbers like 10, 14, 15, 21, 30 are composite and can be broken down further. Fully factoring always yields the same unique set of primes.
  > Source: The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.html
