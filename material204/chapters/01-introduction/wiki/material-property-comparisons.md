# Comparing Material Properties

## Definition
Material property charts compare classes of materials (metals, ceramics, polymers, composites) against measurable properties such as density, elastic modulus, and tensile strength, often on logarithmic scales.
> Source: 15.PNG

## Key Concepts
- **Density:** On a logarithmic density chart, metals (platinum, silver, copper, iron/steel, titanium, aluminum, magnesium) generally show the highest density values; ceramics (ZrO₂, SiC, Si₃N₄, glass, concrete) and composites (GFRC, CFRC, woods) occupy a wide middle range; polymers (PTFE, PVC, PS, PE, rubber) generally show the lowest density values.
  > Source: 15.PNG
- **Elastic modulus (stiffness):** On a logarithmic stiffness chart, metals (tungsten, iron/steel, titanium, aluminum, magnesium) and ceramics (SiC, Al₂O₃, Si₃N₄, ZrO₂, glass, concrete) show the highest stiffness values; composites (CFRC, GFRC, woods) occupy a wide range; polymers (PVC, PS, nylon, PTFE, PE, rubbers) generally show the lowest stiffness values.
  > Source: 16.PNG
- **Tensile strength:** On a logarithmic tensile strength chart, metals (steel alloys, Cu/Ti alloys, aluminum alloys, gold), ceramics (Si₃N₄, Al₂O₃, SiC, glass), polymers (nylon, PS, PVC, PE, PTFE), and composites (CFRC, GFRC, woods) each span a range of strength values, with composites and metal/ceramic classes reaching the highest strengths.
  > Source: 17.PNG
- **Two-property optimization (Ashby map):** An Ashby map plots stiffness (elastic/Young's modulus, GPa) against density (kg/m³) on logarithmic axes, grouping materials into regions for technical ceramics, non-technical ceramics, composites, metals, natural materials, polymers, foams, and elastomers — used to compare and select materials based on two properties simultaneously.
  > Source: 18.PNG
