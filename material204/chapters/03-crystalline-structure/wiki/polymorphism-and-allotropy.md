# Polymorphism and Allotropy

## Definition

**Two or more** distinct crystal structures for the same material (allotropy/polymorphism).
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 43)

Same element + different crystal structure = allotropy.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 44)

## Key Concepts

- **Processing dependence:** Materials can display varying crystal structures depending on processing routes.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 50)

- **The iron system:** iron changes crystal structure with temperature.

  | Temperature range | Phase | Structure |
  |---|---|---|
  | Above 1538 °C | liquid | — |
  | 1394 °C to 1538 °C | δ-Fe | BCC |
  | 912 °C to 1394 °C | γ-Fe | FCC |
  | Below 912 °C | α-Fe | BCC |
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 43)

- **Phase names:** at 912 °C, iron transforms on heating from BCC (α phase, called ferrite phase) to FCC (γ phase, called austenite phase): BCC ferrite → FCC austenite.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 44)

- **Accompanying changes:** the transformation is accompanied by a change in the atomic radius of Fe — from r_BCC = 0.12584 nm to r_FCC = 0.12894 nm — and also a change in density (and volume).
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 44)
