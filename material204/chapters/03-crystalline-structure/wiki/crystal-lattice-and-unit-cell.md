# Crystal Lattice and Unit Cell

## Definition

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Crystal Lattice | Repeating points in space (empty framework) |
| Crystal Structure | Lattice + atoms arranged at lattice points |
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 6)

The smallest unit of the crystalline structure is a **Unit Cell**.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 5)

## Key Concepts

- **Lattice is the framework, structure is the framework plus atoms:** the lattice by itself is an empty framework of repeating points; the crystal structure is that lattice with atoms arranged at its lattice points.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 6)

- **Unit cell as the repeating building block:** the slide shows a single unit cell highlighted inside a larger crystal lattice, illustrating that the lattice is built by repeating the unit cell in space.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 6)

- **Lattice constants:** *a*, *b*, and *c* are the lattice constants.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 7)

## Examples

- **Convocation hall analogy (UCalgary):** Chairs are the lattice points, students are the atoms, and the crystal structure is the whole arrangement.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 6)

- **Physical toolkit:** 3D-printed BCC, FCC, and HCP unit-cell models with matching frames are used in the studio so that whole atoms can be compared with the portions that lie inside the unit cell. The split surfaces of the models show the (111) and (101) planes, which are covered in a later session.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 8)

## Common Misconceptions

- An atom is not physically cut into pieces. The unit-cell boundary divides its *contribution* for accounting purposes only.
