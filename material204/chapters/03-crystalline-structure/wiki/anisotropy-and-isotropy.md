# Anisotropy and Isotropy

## Definition

**Anisotropy** — Property value depends on crystallographic direction of measurement. Anisotropy is the directionality dependence of properties.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 40, 50)

**Isotropy / isotropic** — Having identical values in ALL directions. Isotropic materials are independent of direction of measurement.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 40, 50)

## Key Concepts

- **Anisotropy is observed in single crystals:** single crystal → one preferred crystal orientation → direction-dependent properties → anisotropy.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 40)

- **Polycrystals may be either:** properties may or may not vary with direction.
  - If grains are randomly oriented: properties are **isotropic** (E_poly iron = 210 GPa).
  - If grains are **textured** (e.g., deformed grains have preferential crystallographic orientation): properties are **anisotropic**.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 41)

- **General result:** Polycrystals show isotropic material properties and are the result of most casting and fabrication routes for metal synthesis.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 50)

## Examples

- **Modulus of elasticity (E) in BCC iron:** E(edge) ≠ E(diagonal).
  - E (diagonal) = 273 GPa
  - E (edge) = 125 GPa
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 40)

- **Randomly oriented polycrystalline iron:** E_poly iron = 210 GPa, a single direction-independent value.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 41)
