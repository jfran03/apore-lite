# Crystalline vs Amorphous Solids

## Definition

**Crystalline solids:** particles are arranged in an orderly, repeating three-dimensional network called a *Crystal Lattice*. The smallest unit of this structure is a *Unit Cell*. Strong intermolecular forces lead to minimal space between atoms, resulting in high melting and boiling points. The structure exhibits *long-range order*, meaning the atomic arrangement repeats over large distances.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 5)

**Amorphous solids:** lack a well-defined, geometric shape and are non-crystalline. They do not have sharp edges like crystalline solids.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 5)

Noncrystalline (amorphous) solid materials lack a systematic and regular arrangement of atoms over a large distance.

## Key Concepts

- **Why crystal structure matters:** All materials are made of atoms arranged in specific patterns. The chapter opens with two real-world failures attributed to structure: the Titanic shipwreck (1912), a brittle failure of steel in cold water, and gas pipeline failure in homes, a brittle fracture of steel (e.g., in North America in the 20th century).
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 3)

- **Three states of atomic order:** the slides distinguish three arrangements side by side — crystalline (single crystal), polycrystalline, and amorphous.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 5, 38)

  | Arrangement | Periodicity |
  |---|---|
  | Single crystal | Periodic across the whole volume |
  | Polycrystal | Periodic across each grain |
  | Amorphous | Not periodic |
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 38)

- **Long-range order:** the defining feature of the crystalline state — the atomic arrangement repeats over large distances.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 5)

- **Tendency to crystallize:** Atoms tend to organize in crystalline structures in metals and other materials. The diverse variety of atoms and the difficulty for polymer chains to move makes this more difficult for organic molecules.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 50)
