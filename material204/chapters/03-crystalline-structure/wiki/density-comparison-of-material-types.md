# Density Comparison of Material Types

## Definition

In general: **ρ_metals > ρ_ceramics > ρ_polymers**.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 37)

## Key Concepts

- **Why metals are densest:** Metals have close-packing (metallic bonding) and often large atomic masses.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 37)

- **Why ceramics are lighter:** Ceramics have often lighter elements.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 37)

- **Why polymers are lightest:** Polymers have low packing density (often amorphous) and lighter elements (C, H, O).
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 37)

- **Composites:** Composites have moderate to low densities.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 37)

- **Alloys vs composites:** An alloy must have a minimum of one metal in its composition, while composites don't have any metal components.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 37)

## Examples

- Materials placed on the density chart by group (data based on Table B1, Callister):
  - Metals/Alloys (highest, roughly 2–20+ g/cm³): Platinum, Gold, W, Tantalum, Silver, Mo, Cu, Ni, Steels, Tin, Zinc, Titanium, Aluminum, Magnesium
  - Graphite/Ceramics/Semiconductors: Zirconia, Al oxide, Diamond, Si nitride, Glass-soda, Concrete, Silicon, Graphite
  - Polymers (roughly 1–2 g/cm³): PTFE, Silicone, PVC, PET, PS, PE
  - Composites/fibers: Glass fibers, GFRE, Carbon fibers, CFRE, Aramid fibers, AFRE, Wood
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 37)

- GFRE, CFRE, and AFRE are Glass, Carbon, and Aramid Fiber-Reinforced Epoxy composites (values based on 60% volume fraction of aligned fibers in an epoxy matrix).
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 37)
