# Single Crystals and Polycrystals

## Definition

**Single crystal:** when the periodic arrangement of atoms (crystal structure) extends without interruption throughout the entire specimen. Atoms repeat almost perfectly for the entire material.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 38, 42)

**Polycrystalline:** most crystalline solids are composed of many small crystals or grains ≡ POLYCRYSTALLINE. Atoms repeat within a grain; grains are separated by **grain boundaries**, where the repeating pattern changes direction.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 39, 42)

## Key Concepts

- **Periodicity comparison:**

  | Type | Periodicity |
  |---|---|
  | Single crystal | Periodic across the whole volume |
  | Polycrystal | Periodic across each grain |
  | Amorphous | Not periodic |
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 38)

- **Solidification of a polycrystalline material** proceeds in four stages:
  - (a) small crystalline nuclei
  - (b) growth of crystallites
  - (c) formation of grains of irregular shapes
  - (d) grain boundaries
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 39)

- **Grain boundaries** are where the repeating pattern changes direction between adjacent grains.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 42)

- **Processing link:** Polycrystals show isotropic material properties and are the result of most casting and fabrication routes for metal synthesis.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 50)
