# Atomic Packing Factor (APF)

## Definition

> APF = (Volume of atoms in unit cell) / (Volume of unit cell), assuming hard spheres.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 11)

The packing fraction refers to the fraction of the total volume in a crystal structure that is occupied by atoms. It helps in understanding how efficiently the atoms are packed in a given structure.
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 12)

## Key Concepts

- **What it measures:** essentially a measure of the density of the crystal structure, or how many atoms we have fit into a single unit cell. The higher the packing density, typically the more the atoms of a material will form into this material.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 11)

- **Void fraction:** Void Fraction = 1 − APF.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 11)

- **Hard-sphere assumption:** the APF definition explicitly assumes hard spheres.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 11)

- **Coordination number drives packing:** a major factor affecting how efficiently atoms pack is the coordination number — the number of nearest-neighbor atoms surrounding an atom.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 21)

- **APF across structures:**

  | Structure | APF expression | Value |
  |---|---|---|
  | Simple cubic | [(4/3)πR³ × 1] / (8R³) = π/6 | ≈ 0.52 |
  | BCC | [(4/3)πR³ × 2] / (4R/√3)³ | ≈ 0.68 |
  | FCC | [(4/3)πR³ × 4] / (2√2R)³ = π/(3√2) | ≈ 0.74 |
  | HCP | 8πR³ / (24√2 R³) = π/(3√2) | ≈ 0.7405 |
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 21, 29)

- **Consequence of low packing:** simple cubic is rare due to low packing density.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 10)

- **FCC is the most efficient of the common metal structures:** FCC has the highest packing density.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 50)
