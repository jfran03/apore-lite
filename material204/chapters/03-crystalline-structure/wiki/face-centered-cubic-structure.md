# Face-Centered Cubic (FCC) Structure

## Definition

The FCC unit cell has eight corner atoms (N_c = 8) + six face atoms (N_f = 6) + no interior atoms (N_i = 0).
> Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 17)

## Key Concepts

- **Atoms per unit cell:** N = N_i + N_f/2 + N_c/8 = 0 + 6/2 + 8/8 = **4** (6 face × 1/2 + 8 corners × 1/8).
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 17, 18, 21)

- **Coordination number = 12.** Each corner atom of the unit cell is in contact with 12 face-centered atoms from surrounding unit cells. The slide breaks the 12 down as: four corner atoms on that same top face (4) + four side-face-centred atoms in the cube below (4) + four side-face-centred atoms in the adjoining cube above (4) = 12.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 18, 21)

- **Where atoms touch:** atoms touch each other along the face diagonals.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 16, 18, 32)

- **Lattice constant:** √2 a = 4R, so a = 4R/√2 = 2√2 R. Derived from a² + a² = (4R)² → 2a² = 16R².
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 19, 21, 32)

- **Volume of unit cell:** V = a³ = (4r/√2)³ = 64r³/(2√2). Equivalently V_C = 16R³√2.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 19, 34)

- **Volume of atoms in unit cell:** 4 × (4/3)πr³ = (16/3)πr³.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 19)

- **Atomic packing factor:** APF = [(4/3)πR³ × 4] / (2√2R)³ = π/(3√2) ≈ **0.74**.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 19, 21)

- **Highest packing density:** FCC has the highest packing density of the common metal crystal structures.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 50)

## Examples

- Metals with the FCC structure named in the slides: Al, Cu, Au, Pb, Ni, Pt, Ag.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slide 18)

- Iridium (Ir) has an FCC crystal structure (worked Example Problem 6).
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 33, 34)

- γ-Fe (austenite) is FCC between 912 °C and 1394 °C.
  > Source: F26-D2L-Chapter 3 - Crystalline Structure-Part 1.pdf (slides 43, 44)

## Common Misconceptions
