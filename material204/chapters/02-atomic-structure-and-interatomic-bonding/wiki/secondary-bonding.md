# Secondary Bonding (Van der Waals Bonding)

## Definition
Secondary bonding arises from attractive forces between dipoles.
> Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 22

## Key Concepts
- **Fluctuating dipoles:** arise from asymmetric electron clouds around atoms/molecules; example given is liquid H₂, where secondary bonding forms between adjacent H₂ molecules due to fluctuating dipole attraction.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 22
- **Permanent dipoles:** secondary bonding also occurs between molecules with a permanent dipole (general case: a molecule with a + end and a - end bonds to another such molecule via secondary bonding). Examples given: liquid HCl (secondary bonding between H-Cl molecules) and a linear polymer molecule (secondary bonding between chains).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 22
- **Hydrogen bonding:** a special type of secondary bonding where hydrogen is one of the constituents. Examples: HF, H₂O, NH₃. The magnitude of hydrogen bonding is generally higher than other types of secondary bonds, and melting/boiling temperatures for HF, H₂O, and NH₃ are correspondingly high.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 23
- **Directionality:** some secondary bonding is directional, existing only because of the location of a dipole moment (contrasted with ionic/metallic non-directional bonding).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 24
- **Prevalence and relative strength:** secondary bonding exists between virtually all atoms or molecules, but its presence may be obscured if any of the three primary bonding types (covalent, ionic, metallic) is present. Secondary (van der Waals) bonds are weaker than the primary bonding types but nonetheless influence the physical properties of some materials.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 31

## Examples
- Liquid H₂ (fluctuating dipole secondary bonding between H₂ molecules).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 22
- Liquid HCl (permanent dipole secondary bonding between HCl molecules).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 22
- Water (H₂O): intramolecular polar covalent bonding within a water molecule, alongside intermolecular hydrogen bonding between separate water molecules.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 23
- Bonding energy/melting temperature data: Ar (7.7 kJ/mol, −189°C @ 69 kPa), Kr (11.7 kJ/mol, −158°C @ 73.2 kPa), CH₄ (18 kJ/mol, −182°C), Cl₂ (31 kJ/mol, −101°C).
