# Interatomic Bonding Overview

## Definition
Atoms bond due to how electrons distribute around nuclei. Interatomic bonding is divided into primary bonding (ionic, covalent, metallic) and secondary bonding (van der Waals bonding, including hydrogen bonding).
> Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slides 2, 14

## Key Concepts
- **Primary bonding:** ionic, covalent, and metallic bonding.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 14
- **Secondary bonding (van der Waals bonding):** bonding from atomic or molecular dipole interactions, including hydrogen bonding.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 14
- **Directional bonding:** covalent bonding and some secondary bonding. Electrons or shared electrons are localized in a specific area between atoms; the bond can only exist because of the location of electrons or a dipole moment.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 24
- **Non-directional bonding:** ionic and metallic bonding. Electrons cannot be localized in a specific location between atoms; bonding forces are the same in all directions around the atom.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 24
- **Intramolecular vs. intermolecular forces:** intramolecular forces hold atoms together within a molecule; intermolecular forces bond molecules together.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 26
- **Relative strength of intramolecular forces (strongest to weakest):** metallic bond (metal cations to delocalized electrons) > ionic bond (cations to anions) > polar covalent bond (partially charged cation to partially charged anion) > nonpolar covalent bond (nuclei to shared electrons).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 27
- **Intermolecular forces and boiling/melting points:** boiling and melting points of compounds depend on the type and strength of intermolecular forces present. Rule of thumb: the stronger the intermolecular forces, the more energy is required to break them; ionic and polar covalent compounds have higher boiling and melting points.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 28
