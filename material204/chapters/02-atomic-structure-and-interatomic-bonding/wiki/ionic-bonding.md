# Ionic Bonding

## Definition
Ionic bonding occurs between positive and negative ions, requires electron transfer, and requires a large difference in electronegativity between the bonding atoms.
> Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 15

## Key Concepts
- **Mechanism:** a metal atom donates electrons and becomes a stable cation; a non-metal atom accepts electrons and becomes a stable anion. This occurs when the atoms have dissimilar electronegativities.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 16
- **Coulombic attraction:** the resulting cation and anion are held together by Coulombic (electrostatic) attraction between the positive and negative charges.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 15
- **Example — MgO:** Mg (metal, unstable) and O (nonmetal, unstable) exchange an electron; Mg becomes a stable cation (Mg²⁺) and O becomes a stable anion (O²⁻ anion).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 15
- **Ionization process example — MgO:** Mg (1s²2s²2p⁶3s², or [Ne]3s²) donates its 3s² electrons to O (1s²2s²2p⁴), forming Mg²⁺ (1s²2s²2p⁶, or [Ne]) and O²⁻ (1s²2s²2p⁶, or [Ne]).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 16
- **Ionic bonding as predominant bonding in ceramics:** examples of ionically bonded compounds include NaCl, MgO, CaF₂, and CsCl.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 18
- **Notation — Lewis structure:** to represent an ionic compound (e.g., NaCl), write the element symbol, determine the number of valence electrons from the element's group (e.g., chlorine is in group 7, so it has 7 valence electrons), and draw dots representing electrons around the symbol, starting at the right and going counter-clockwise, pairing electrons as needed.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 34

## Examples
- MgO (magnesium donates 2 electrons to oxygen).
