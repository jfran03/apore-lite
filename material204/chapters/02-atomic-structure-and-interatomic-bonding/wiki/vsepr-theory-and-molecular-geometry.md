# VSEPR Theory and Molecular Geometry

## Definition
VSEPR (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion) theory states that valence electron pairs surrounding an atom tend to repel each other; greater repulsion means higher energy and less stability. VSEPR predicts the molecular geometry that minimizes repulsion, and is used to predict 3D molecular geometry.

## Key Concepts
- **Procedure for predicting molecular geometry using VSEPR:** (1) draw the Lewis structure, (2) count electron groups (bonding pairs and lone pairs) around the central atom, (3) determine the electron geometry, (4) determine the molecular geometry based on the number of bonding pairs vs. lone pairs.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 33
- **VSEPR nomenclature (AXₘEₙ):** A = central atom, X = bonding atom, E = non-bonded electrons, m = integer number of bonding atoms, n = integer number of non-bonded electron pairs.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 35
- **Molecular shapes by formula type:** AX₂ → planar geometry, 180° bond angle. AX₃ → trigonal planar geometry, 120° bond angle. AX₄ → tetrahedral geometry, 109.5° bond angle.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 36
- **Predicting molecular shape distortions:** VSEPR predicts distortions by ranking repulsion strength and considering the space each electron-pair type occupies. Repulsion strength, from greatest to weakest: lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bonding pair > bonding pair–bonding pair. Order of sizes, from largest to smallest: lone pair > triple bond > double bond > single bond.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 38
- **Unpaired vs. bonded electron space:** unpaired valence electrons often take slightly more space than bonded electrons, which may change bond angles slightly. Double and triple bonds have stronger repulsive forces than single bonds, similar to the effect of lone electron pairs.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 39
