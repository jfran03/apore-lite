# Periodic Table and Electronegativity

## Definition
The periodic table organizes elements as metals, nonmetals, or intermediate, and indicates tendencies to give up or accept electrons.
> Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 10

## Key Concepts
- **Electropositive elements:** readily give up electrons to become positive ions. Group IA gives up 1 electron, Group IIA gives up 2 electrons, and elements in the group left of the transition metals give up 3 electrons.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 10
- **Electronegative elements:** readily acquire electrons to become negative ions. Elements accept 2 electrons or accept 1 electron depending on group; inert gases neither give up nor accept electrons.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 10
- **Electronegativity:** ranges from 0.7 to 4.0. Large electronegativity values indicate a tendency to acquire electrons. Electronegativity decreases moving toward the lower-left of the periodic table (e.g., Fr = 0.7, Cs = 0.7) and increases toward the upper-right (e.g., F = 4.0, O = 3.5).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 11

## Examples
- Specific electronegativity values given: H = 2.1, Li = 1.0, Be = 1.5, C = 2.5, N = 3.0, O = 3.5, F = 4.0, Na = 0.9, Mg = 1.2, Cl = 3.0, Fe = 1.8, Cu = 1.9.
