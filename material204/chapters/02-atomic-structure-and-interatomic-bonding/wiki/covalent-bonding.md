# Covalent Bonding

## Definition
Covalent bonding occurs between atoms with similar electronegativities, which share electrons. Bonds involve valence electrons — normally s and p orbitals are involved.
> Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 18

## Key Concepts
- **Example — H₂:** each hydrogen atom has 1 valence electron and needs 1 more; the electronegativities of the two hydrogen atoms are the same, so each contributes a shared 1s electron, forming a shared electron pair between the two atoms.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 18
- **Bond hybridization (sp³):** carbon can form sp³ hybrid orbitals. This occurs via promotion of an electron from the 2s orbital into the 2p orbital, followed by sp³ hybridization, producing four equivalent 2sp³ orbitals arranged with 109.5° angles between them.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 19
- **Example — CH₄ (sp³ hybrid covalent bonding):** each carbon atom has 4 valence electrons and needs 4 more; each hydrogen atom has 1 valence electron and needs 1 more. Because the electronegativities of C and H are similar, electrons are shared in sp³ hybrid covalent bonds, with regions of orbital overlap between carbon's sp³ orbitals and each hydrogen's 1s orbital.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 20
