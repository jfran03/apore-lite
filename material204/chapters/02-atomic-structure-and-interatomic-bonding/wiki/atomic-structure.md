# Atomic Structure

## Definition
An atom consists of electrons (mass 9.11 x 10⁻³¹ kg), protons, and neutrons (protons and neutrons each ~1.67 x 10⁻²⁷ kg).
> Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 3

## Key Concepts
- **Atomic number:** the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom, which equals the number of electrons in a neutral species.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 3
- **Atomic mass unit (amu):** defined as 1/12 the mass of ¹²C.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 3
- **Atomic weight (A):** the weight of 6.022 x 10²³ molecules or atoms; 1 amu/atom = 1 g/mol. Example values given: C = 12.011, H = 1.008.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 3
- **Electron position:** electrons occupy orbitals, and an electron's position is defined by a probability distribution. Each orbital has a discrete energy level determined by quantum numbers.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 5
- **Quantum numbers:**
  - *n* = principal (shell): designated K, L, M, N, O, corresponding to values 1, 2, 3, 4, etc.
  - *ℓ* = azimuthal (subshell): designated s, p, d, f, corresponding to values 0, 1, 2, 3, …, n-1.
  - *mℓ* = magnetic (number of orbitals): values 1, 3, 5, 7 (ranging -ℓ to +ℓ).
  - *ms* = spin: values ½, -½.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 5
- **Electron energy states:** subshells (1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 3d, 4s, 4p, 4d, …) are arranged in increasing energy, grouped into shells: K-shell (n=1), L-shell (n=2), M-shell (n=3), N-shell (n=4).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 6
- **Electron configuration:** the filling order of subshells for an element, e.g., Hydrogen (1) = 1s¹; Helium (2) = 1s² (stable); Lithium (3) = 1s²2s¹; Neon (10) = 1s²2s²2p⁶ (stable); Argon (18) = 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶ (stable); Krypton (36) = 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p⁶3d¹⁰4s²4p⁶ (stable).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 7
- **Valence electrons:** those in the outer unfilled shells. Filled shells are more stable and require more energy to gain or lose electrons. Valence electrons are available for bonding and tend to determine an atom's chemical properties. Example: Carbon (atomic number 6), configuration 1s² 2s²2p² — the 2s²2p² electrons are the valence electrons.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 8
- **Example — Iron (Fe, atomic number 26):** electron configuration 1s² 2s²2p² 3s²3p⁶ 3d⁶4s² (3d⁶4s² are the valence electrons).
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 9
