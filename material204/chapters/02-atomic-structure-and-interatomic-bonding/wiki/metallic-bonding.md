# Metallic Bonding

## Definition
In metallic bonding, electrons are delocalized to form an "electron cloud."
> Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 21

## Key Concepts
- **Structure:** positively charged ion cores are arranged in a lattice, surrounded by a "sea of valence electrons" that is delocalized across the whole structure rather than bound to individual atoms.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 21
- **Relative bond strength:** the metallic bond is ranked as the strongest of the intramolecular forces, formed via the attraction of metal cations to delocalized electrons.
  > Source: before lecture-Chapter 2 - Atomic Structure and Interatomic Bonding.pdf, slide 27

## Examples
- Bonding energy/melting temperature data: Hg (62 kJ/mol, −39°C), Al (330 kJ/mol, 660°C), Ag (285 kJ/mol, 962°C), W (850 kJ/mol, 3414°C).
