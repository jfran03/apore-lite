## Q028

**Focus Area:** amu-to-gram conversion
**Question:** Starting from one mole of carbon-12, show how 1 amu is converted to grams.
**Answer:** One mole of carbon-12 is 12 g containing 6.022 × 10²³ atoms, and each carbon-12 atom has a mass of 12 amu. Dividing both relationships by 12 gives 1 amu = 1 g ÷ (6.022 × 10²³) = 0.166 × 10⁻²³ g ≈ 1.66 × 10⁻²⁴ g. The numerical bridge is 12 amu per atom ↔ 12 g per mole. (See [Atomic Mass, Atomic Weight, and the Mole](../wiki/atomic-mass-atomic-weight-and-the-mole.md).)

## Q029
**Status:** active
**Type:** short-answer
**Difficulty:** introductory
**Topic:** atomic-mass-atomic-weight-and-the-mole
**Focus Area:** the mole as a counting unit
**Question:** If you have 0.5 mol of a substance, how many atoms do you have, and why is the mole called a counting unit?
**Answer:** 0.5 mol contains 0.5 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.011 × 10²³ atoms. A mole is a counting unit in the same way a dozen is — a dozen = 12 objects, a mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles — the idea is the same, only the size of the counting group differs. Because a mole is a number unit (like kilo = 10³ or mega = 10⁶), it carries no physical unit such as gram or newton. (See [Atomic Mass, Atomic Weight, and the Mole](../wiki/atomic-mass-atomic-weight-and-the-mole.md).)

**Answer:** σ is a characteristic distance — the separation at which the potential energy is zero, V(σ) = 0, i.e. where the Lennard-Jones potential crosses zero. r₀ is the separation at which the potential reaches its minimum. They are not equal: r₀ = 2^(1/6)σ ≈ 1.122σ. (See [Bonding Forces and Energies](../wiki/bonding-forces-and-energies.md).)

## Q050
**Status:** active
**Type:** short-answer
**Difficulty:** advanced
**Topic:** bonding-forces-and-energies
**Focus Area:** deriving the equilibrium separation
**Question:** Starting from V(r) = 4ε[(σ/r)¹² − (σ/r)⁶], derive the equilibrium separation r₀.
**Answer:** Differentiate: dV/dr = 4ε[−12σ¹²/r¹³ + 6σ⁶/r⁷]. Set the derivative to zero at r = r₀; since ε > 0, 4ε ≠ 0 and both sides can be divided by it, giving 12σ¹²/r₀¹³ = 6σ⁶/r₀⁷. Multiply by r₀¹³: 12σ¹² = 6σ⁶r₀⁶, so r₀⁶ = 2σ⁶. Taking the positive sixth root (separation must be positive), r₀ = ⁶√2 × σ ≈ 1.122σ. (See [Bonding Forces and Energies](../wiki/bonding-forces-and-energies.md).)

## Q051
**Status:** active
**Type:** short-answer
**Difficulty:** advanced
**Topic:** bonding-forces-and-energies
**Focus Area:** minimum potential energy and well depth
**Question:** Substituting r₀ back into the Lennard-Jones potential, what is the potential energy at equilibrium, and how does it relate to the depth of the potential well?
**Answer:** Since r₀⁶ = 2σ⁶, (σ/r₀)⁶ = 1/2 and (σ/r₀)¹² = 1/4, so E(r₀) = 4ε[1/4 − 1/2] = 4ε[−1/4] = −ε. The well depth is +ε while the energy at the minimum is −ε. (See [Bonding Forces and Energies](../wiki/bonding-forces-and-energies.md).)

## Q052
**Status:** active
**Type:** conceptual
**Difficulty:** intermediate
**Topic:** bonding-forces-and-energies
**Focus Area:** meaning of ε as bond energy
**Question:** What does ε represent physically, and what does a larger ε imply about the bond?
**Answer:** ε is the depth of the potential-energy well and is the bond energy — the amount of energy required to separate two bonded atoms from their equilibrium spacing to an infinite distance. A deeper well means more energy is required to separate the atoms, so a larger ε means a stronger interatomic interaction: a shallow well gives weaker bonding, a deep well stronger bonding. (See [Bonding Forces and Energies](../wiki/bonding-forces-and-energies.md).)

## Q053
**Status:** active
**Type:** conceptual
**Difficulty:** advanced
**Topic:** bonding-forces-and-energies
**Focus Area:** sign convention for bond energy
**Question:** The potential energy at the equilibrium distance is negative, yet bond energy is customarily quoted as a positive number. Explain why.
**Answer:** The potential energy at the equilibrium distance must be negative because positive work must be done to break the bond and liberate the two atoms. The work done against the interatomic force in taking the atoms from r₀ out to infinity is a positive quantity, and bond energy is customarily stated as that positive number even though the potential energy at r₀ is negative. ε is therefore a positive quantity, called the depth of the potential well because V looks like a well when plotted against r. (See [Bonding Forces and Energies](../wiki/bonding-forces-and-energies.md).)

## Q054
**Status:** active
**Type:** true-false
**Difficulty:** intermediate
**Topic:** bonding-forces-and-energies
**Focus Area:** type of equilibrium
**Question:** True or false: the "equilibrium distance" between two atoms under a Lennard-Jones potential refers to chemical equilibrium.
**Answer:** False. It refers to mechanical equilibrium — not chemical equilibrium nor thermal equilibrium. Mechanical equilibrium is achieved when the force acting between the two atoms is zero. (See [Bonding Forces and Energies](../wiki/bonding-forces-and-energies.md).)

## Q055
**Status:** active
**Type:** short-answer
**Difficulty:** intermediate
**Topic:** bonding-forces-and-energies
**Focus Area:** Coulombic attractive energy
**Question:** Write the expression for the attractive energy between two isolated ions, define the constant A, and give the values of the quantities it contains.
**Answer:** E_A = −A/r (Eq. 2.9), where A = (1/4πε₀)(|Z₁|e)(|Z₂|e) (Eq. 2.10). Here ε₀ is the permittivity of a vacuum (8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m), |Z₁| and |Z₂| are the absolute values of the valences of the two ion types, and e is the electronic charge (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C). The attractive bonding forces are coulombic: positive and negative ions, by virtue of their net electrical charge, attract one another. (See [Bonding Forces and Energies](../wiki/bonding-forces-and-energies.md).)
