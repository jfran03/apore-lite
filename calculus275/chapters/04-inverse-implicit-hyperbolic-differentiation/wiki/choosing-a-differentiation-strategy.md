# Choosing a Differentiation Strategy

## Definition
The outer appearance of a problem usually indicates the method. Inverse-function differentiation and implicit differentiation are both consequences of the chain rule; hyperbolic functions are a new family of exponential-based functions whose derivatives mimic familiar trigonometric patterns, and their inverses are differentiated by the same reciprocal-slope principle used for inverse trigonometric functions.
> Source: MATH275-Week04-Lecture-Notes.pdf

## Key Concepts
- **A value of (f⁻¹)':** find the corresponding point on f, then take the reciprocal of f' there.
  > Source: MATH275-Week04-Lecture-Notes.pdf
- **arcsin(g(x)), artanh(g(x)), etc.:** use the derivative formula for the outer inverse function, then multiply by g'(x).
  > Source: MATH275-Week04-Lecture-Notes.pdf
- **An equation mixing x and y:** differentiate both sides and attach y' to every differentiated y-term.
  > Source: MATH275-Week04-Lecture-Notes.pdf
- **sinh, cosh, tanh, …:** use the hyperbolic derivative formulas exactly as trig derivative formulas, keeping the different identities straight.
  > Source: MATH275-Week04-Lecture-Notes.pdf
- **An inverse hyperbolic formula:** use the inverse-function derivative rule; to obtain an explicit form, rewrite with exponentials and solve for eʸ.
  > Source: MATH275-Week04-Lecture-Notes.pdf

## Examples
- Exit-check items pair one problem with each idea: (f⁻¹)' from f(2) = −1, f'(2) = 5; y = arccos(2x − 1); an implicit relation x² + 3xy − y² = 5; the identity cosh² x − sinh² x = 1; y = tanh(x³); y = arsinh(4x).
  > Source: MATH275-Week04-Lecture-Notes.pdf

## Common Misconceptions
- **Three notation traps:** (1) f⁻¹ is an inverse function, but x⁻¹ = 1/x is a reciprocal power; (2) sin⁻¹ x means arcsin x, while 1/sin x = csc x; (3) sinh⁻¹ x is commonly used for arsinh x, not for csch x — context and parentheses matter.
