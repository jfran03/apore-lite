# Power (Absorbed vs. Supplied)

## Definition
The actual direction of current and actual voltage polarity indicate whether a circuit element is absorbing or supplying power.
> Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf

## Key Concepts
- **Power absorbed:** If the actual current direction enters the + terminal of the actual voltage polarity for an element A (current flows into the + side), power is absorbed by A.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Power supplied:** If the actual current direction enters the − terminal of the actual voltage polarity for an element A (i.e. current flows in the direction of voltage rise, from − to +), power is supplied by A.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Rule of thumb from worked example:** Actual current in the direction of a voltage rise means the element supplies power; actual current in the direction of a voltage drop means the element absorbs power.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf

## Examples
- In "Yani's cheap plastic circuit example" (a 12V battery connected to a 12Ω load), after solving, i = 1 A actual current flows in the direction of voltage rise through the battery, so the battery supplies power. Through the resistor, the actual current flows in the direction of voltage drop, so the resistor absorbs power.
