# Current

## Definition
Electric current: rate of flow of electrical charge. i(t) = dq(t)/dt, where i(t) is current in Amperes (A), q(t) is charge in Coulombs (C), and t is time in seconds. e.g. 1 A = 1 C/s.
> Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf

## Key Concepts
- **Electrical charge:** The concept of electrical charge is the basis for describing all electrical phenomena. Charge exists in discrete quantities at integer multiples of 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ Coulomb (the charge of an electron).
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **i(t) as equivalent positive charge flow:** i(t) is a measure of equivalent positive charge flow. E.g., i(t) = 2 A corresponds to 2 C/s of electrons flowing in the opposite direction, or 12.48×10¹⁸ electrons/sec.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Finding charge from current:** Given i(t), charge can be found via q(t) = ∫[t₀ to t] i(t) dt + q(t₀), where q(t₀) is the initial value at t₀.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Reference direction:** We normally assign a reference direction for current. If the current direction is not known, the reference direction can be chosen arbitrarily. The actual direction is indicated by the sign of the current once the circuit is analyzed (solved). E.g., if i_A is calculated to be -5 A, the actual current in A is 5 A in the opposite (right to left) direction.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **DC and AC current:** Direct Current (DC) has a constant value over time (e.g. a battery). Alternating Current (AC) varies over time, e.g. following a sinusoidal shape (e.g. house outlets).
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Branch current notation:** i_A denotes the branch current through element A in the assigned reference direction. With terminal references, i_mn denotes current flowing from terminal m to terminal n through element A; i_nm denotes current in the opposite direction. i_mn = -i_nm.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf

## Examples
- If i(t) = 2 A in a conductor, this corresponds to 2 C/s of electrons flowing in the direction opposite to i(t), or equivalently 12.48×10¹⁸ electrons/sec.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- For elements A and B with assigned reference directions i_A and i_B, if analysis yields i_A = -5 A, the actual current in A is 5 A, flowing right to left (opposite the assumed reference direction).
