# Voltage

## Definition
Voltage: energy transferred to/from a circuit element per unit of charge flowing through it. Also called potential difference. v(t) = dw(t)/dq(t), where v(t) is voltage in Volts (V), w(t) is energy in Joules (J), and q(t) is charge in Coulombs (C). e.g. 1 V = 1 J/C.

## Key Concepts
- **Reference polarities:** For analysis purposes, reference polarities can be assigned (if not already given) and chosen arbitrarily. If the actual polarity is opposite to the assigned reference, the value of V will be negative.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Common notation:** V_A denotes the voltage across element A with a marked reference polarity (e.g. − on one terminal, + on the other). With terminal references, V_nm marks the first subscript on the (+) terminal — e.g. V_nm has n as the + terminal and m as the − terminal. V_A and V_nm (with matching polarity assignment) produce the same answer. V_mn = −V_nm.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Relationship to power:** The actual direction of current and actual voltage polarity together indicate whether an element is absorbing or supplying power (see [Power](power.md)).
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
