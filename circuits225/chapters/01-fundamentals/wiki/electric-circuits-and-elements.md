# Electric Circuits and Circuit Elements

## Definition
Electric circuit: interconnection of circuit elements in a closed path by conductors.
> Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf

## Key Concepts
- **Circuit elements (example set):** A voltage source (supplier of energy), a resistor, a capacitor, and an inductor connected together in a closed path by conductors form an example circuit.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Conductor:** A conductor is a wire, cable, etc. used to connect circuit elements together in a closed path.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Capacitor:** Can store energy in an electric field.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Inductor:** Can store energy in a magnetic field.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Ideal conductor:** Between two circuit elements, an ideal conductor has i flowing through it with V = 0 across it. This is called a "short circuit" — the two points it connects are "shorted" together.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Open circuit:** The absence of a conductor between circuit elements is an open circuit (a gap). In an open circuit, i = 0, and the voltage V across the gap is unknown until the circuit is solved.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Ideal conductors can be reshaped:** Since all conductors are ideal, they can be shortened or lengthened as much as needed, as long as the electrical connections between circuit elements remain the same.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf

## Examples
- A circuit with elements A, B, and C: conductors connect terminals A₁, B₁, and C₁ together, and terminals A₂, B₂, and C₂ together. This circuit is electrically equivalent to a redrawn version where A, B, and C all share a common node on each side (B₁/A₁/C₁ on one side, C₂/A₂/B₂ on the other), because ideal conductors can be reshaped without changing the electrical connections.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
