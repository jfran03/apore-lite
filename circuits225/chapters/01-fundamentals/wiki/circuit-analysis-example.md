# Circuit Analysis Example — Yani's Cheap Plastic Circuit

## Definition
A worked example ("Yani's cheap plastic circuit example") illustrating a car battery (12 V) connected to headlights (modeled as a 12 Ω load), used to demonstrate assigning unknowns, reference directions/polarities, and why voltage/current values matter.
> Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf

## Key Concepts
- **What we don't know initially:** Voltage across the lights, current through the lights, current through the battery.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Arbitrary assumptions:** We can assume arbitrary directions/polarities for all unknowns before solving the circuit.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Why voltage/current matter:** Lights require a certain voltage to operate properly, so solving the circuit tells us if that voltage is provided. All elements (battery, light, conductors) have a current rating (maximum current before overheating/melting/catching fire), so solving the circuit confirms current does not exceed that rating. Once voltage and current for an element are known, power and energy consumed/supplied can be calculated.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf
- **Result of solving:** After solving the circuit, i = 1 A and the voltage across the 12 Ω load is 12 V.
  > Source: Week 2 - Full.pdf

## Examples
- Circuit: a 12 V source connected to a 12 Ω resistor (representing the headlights). Assumed current direction and assumed voltage polarity (V_R) are marked before solving. After solving, i = 1 A actual current, with the load voltage 12 V; the battery is found to supply power (actual current in direction of voltage rise) and the resistor absorbs power (actual current in direction of voltage drop).
