# Identifying Parallel Connections

## Definition
Watch for parallel connections that may not appear like parallel elements at first glance.
> Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf

## Key Concepts
- **Redraw to reveal the connection:** A circuit can be redrawn so that elements sharing the same two nodes line up visually as parallel branches. The redrawn circuit is the same circuit — only the drawing changed.
  > Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf
- **Trace the nodes, not the picture:** The test is which nodes an element is connected between. Identifying and labelling the nodes (e.g. node 1 and node 2) is what exposes elements that are in parallel despite being drawn in a scattered arrangement.
  > Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf

## Examples
- **Four resistors that do not look parallel:** A circuit is drawn with R₁ and R₄ along the bottom and R₂ and R₃ crossing diagonally through the middle, meeting at a point labelled node 1, with the outer conductor labelled node 2. Tracing the nodes shows every one of R₁, R₂, R₃ and R₄ is connected between node 1 and node 2. Redrawn, the circuit becomes four resistors side by side in parallel between node 2 (top) and node 1 (bottom).
  > Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf

## Common Misconceptions
- Judging series/parallel structure from how the circuit happens to be drawn. The source explicitly warns that parallel connections "may not appear like parallel elements at first glance."
