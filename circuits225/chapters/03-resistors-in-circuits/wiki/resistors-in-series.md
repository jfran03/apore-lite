# Resistors in Series

## Definition
Resistors in series add: a chain of series resistors can be replaced with a single equivalent resistor R_eq = R₁ + R₂ + R₃.
> Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf

## Key Concepts
- **Common current:** Series elements share a common current i.
  > Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf
- **Derivation from KVL and Ohm's Law:** For a source V driving R₁, R₂, R₃ in series — by KVL: −V + V₁ + V₂ + V₃ = 0; by Ohm's Law: V₁ = i·R₁, V₂ = i·R₂, V₃ = i·R₃. Substituting gives −V + iR₁ + iR₂ + iR₃ = 0, so V = i(R₁ + R₂ + R₃), which "looks like" V = iR.
  > Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf
- **The equivalent resistor:** Because V = i(R₁ + R₂ + R₃) has the form V = iR, all the series resistors can be replaced by R_eq = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ without changing what the source sees.
  > Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf
- **Opens the way to analysis:** This appears under "Resistive Circuits," introduced with the remark that we now have all the tools to begin circuit analysis.
  > Source: Week 4 - Full.pdf

## Examples
- **Reduction of a series pair:** In the equivalent-resistance example, a 25 Ω and a 5 Ω resistor connected end to end give R_eq = 30 Ω, and a 30 Ω and 10 Ω pair gives R_eq = 40 Ω.
